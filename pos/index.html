Offline-first

An outage is decided once, not on every tap.

A fetch to a host that is up-but-unreachable — dead WAN, a powered-off LAN server, a captive portal — does not fail fast. It hangs for the OS connect timeout, tens of seconds, and a till pays that three to five times per bill. So the first failure is remembered: every action after it goes straight to the local path in microseconds, until a cooldown elapses and exactly one request is allowed through to test the water.

navigator.onLine is trusted in one direction only. false proves there is no route; true proves nothing. That is why there is a breaker and not a flag.

  • Bounded requestsA read may hang 6 seconds, a write 20. The write timeout is generous on purpose — it should only fire on a dead link.
  • 2s → 5s → 10s → 30sThe breaker's cooldown after the 1st, 2nd, 3rd and 4th consecutive failure. Six parallel failures count as one outage.
  • An aborted write is never re-queuedThe server may have applied it. An unknown outcome goes back to the operator, not into a retry loop.
  • Nothing settles twiceEvery mutation carries a device-generated id, recorded with its effects in one transaction.
  • Same arithmetic offlineAn offline bill and an online one for the same cart agree to the last decimal — one shared tax module, no second implementation.
  • Online-only by designThe ERP, the reports and the assistant need the server. Billing, KOT, printing, tables and PIN login do not.

What the till does.

Cloud POS
Orders, KOT rounds, split bills, table transfer, takeaway, delivery-platform orders and quick bill — on Windows, macOS, Android, iOS and the browser.
Table management
Floor plan across multiple floors, live occupancy and elapsed time per table, table transfer, merge and split of bills, per-table running totals.
Menu management
Categories, items, pricing, per-outlet price overrides and availability, 86-ing an item from the till, recipe-to-ingredient mapping so a sale deducts stock, and per-category KOT routing — including categories that print no KOT at all.
KDS
Kitchen display with live tickets grouped by round and elapsed time, marked ready by the kitchen.
Ticket splitting
One printer, several tickets. Group by printer, by station (name a station beside a category — Beverages to Bar), or one ticket per category. Split tickets print a station banner under the KOT number.
Tenders
Cash, card and UPI are built in because the system reasons about them directly. Everything else — a meal card, a wallet, a credit account, a delivery platform — is configured per outlet and carried under its own code from the till to the report.
Customers
Customer directory searchable by name or phone, with order history, lifetime spend and last visit attached to bills.
Inventory
Stock on hand per location, reorder levels, daily usage log, waste log with reasons, transfers between outlets, and an immutable stock-movement trail — purchase, production, transfer, sale, waste, adjustment, count — with signed quantities and who did it.
Expenses and cash drawer
Outlet expenses by category and vendor with an approval step, vendor payments, purchase log, and a drawer ledger recording every cash movement: sales, top-ups, expenses, vendor and staff payments, withdrawals, adjustments.
Payroll
Staff records, attendance register, monthly payroll runs with days present, earnings, deductions and net payable, advances and settlements, payslips.
Reports
End-of-day and consolidated reporting: sales by outlet, staff, hour, category, order type and tender; discounts and voids; tax summary; P&L with food cost and gross margin. Printable and exportable.
Business day, not calendar day
A venue that closes at 3am gets one business day, one drawer and one close — not two halves that can never be reconciled.

From cart to kitchen to drawer.

Printing, without drivers.

Network thermal printers are spoken to directly over raw TCP on port 9100 — no vendor driver, no print spooler, no OS printer to install. A printer already installed on the machine is used through the OS queue instead: RAW jobs on Windows, lp -o raw on macOS and Linux.

Jobs are serialised per printer and retried, so two cashiers printing at the same moment cannot interleave their bytes into one unreadable ticket. When a split KOT partly fails, the message names the ticket that did not print — 10.0.0.5:9100 (Bar) — not just the printer.
