Step 01 · Setting up the terminal

The terminal, out of the box

A fresh install asks for an admin login once. From then on the device is paired to an outlet, and floor staff never see an email field again.

Step 02 · Setting up the terminal

Paired to an outlet

Pairing binds the device to one outlet — by QR from a manager's phone, or by picking the outlet after an admin login. Its printers, tables, menu and tenders all follow from that.

Step 03 · Setting up the terminal

The roster, not a login screen

The till shows the outlet's staff. There are no shared passwords on the shop floor.

Step 04 · Taking the order

Four digits to start a shift

A cashier taps their name and a 4-digit PIN. The PIN is verified on the device, so a shift can start during an outage.

Step 05 · Taking the order

Straight to the floor

A cashier lands on the floor. A manager lands on the manager portal, and the kitchen lands on the KDS. Nobody chooses an app.

Step 06 · Taking the order

The floor

Every table with its running total and how long it has been occupied. Multiple floors, takeaway and each delivery platform get their own entry point. Here: T-1 occupied five minutes, one bill at 3,412.50; the other eight tables free.

Step 07 · Taking the order

Opening a table

Tapping a table opens its order. Search the menu with Ctrl+F, or type a quantity and an item code straight into the cart.

Step 08 · Taking the order

The cart

Items carry notes and a parcel flag — “Less spicy, well done” rides with the Chicken Tikka to the kitchen. The green block has not reached the kitchen yet: 3,250.00 is sent, 950.00 is still a draft.

Step 09 · Kitchen

Sent

Ctrl+Enter fires the KOT. The floor updates on every device in the outlet at once, and the ticket prints in the kitchen.

Step 10 · Kitchen

A second round

Later courses become a second KOT round. The kitchen only ever sees what is new; the bill keeps the whole order.

Step 11 · Kitchen

The kitchen display

Tickets grouped by round with elapsed time, marked ready by the kitchen. Categories that should not print — a drinks list, say — are routed away from the kitchen entirely, or onto their own ticket at the bar.

Step 12 · The floor moves

Moving a table

A party changes table. The bill, its KOT rounds and its attribution all move with it.

Step 13 · The floor moves

Moved

T-1 is free, T-3 carries the bill. No re-keying, and the kitchen is not re-notified for food it has already made.

Step 14 · Settling

The bill

Printed to the counter's thermal printer — over the network on port 9100, or through any printer the OS already has installed. No drivers.

Step 15 · Settling

Taking payment

Alt+C cash, Alt+D card, Alt+U UPI, Alt+S split, Alt+L pay later. Delivery-platform tenders appear alongside them, configured per outlet.

Step 16 · Settling

Split across tenders

A bill can be settled by several tenders at once. Each keeps its own line all the way to the cash-up — they are never merged.

Step 17 · Settling

Settled

The table is free and the drawer has moved. If the outlet is offline, the bill is complete locally and drains to the server when the link returns.

Step 18 · Settling

History

Every bill of the business day, with its tenders, its edits and who made them. Ctrl+P reprints the last one.

Step 19 · The other ways to sell

Takeaway and delivery

Takeaway and each delivery platform are first-class order types, not a dine-in bill with a note. The platform is chosen at the first tap and carried all the way to the report.

Step 20 · The other ways to sell

Quick bill

F4 for a counter sale: items, tender, print, done — without opening a table.

Step 21 · The manager's day

Live pulse

The outlet right now: sales today 5,344.50 across 3 settled bills, nothing open on the floor, nothing waiting in the kitchen, full menu available.

Step 22 · The manager's day

The drawer

Every cash movement is a ledger line — sales, top-ups, expenses, vendor and staff payments, withdrawals, adjustments. The balance is derived, not guessed.

Step 23 · The manager's day

Stock at the outlet

Stock on hand, reorder levels, the daily usage log and waste with reasons. A sale deducts ingredients through the item's recipe.

Step 24 · The manager's day

The team

Staff, PINs, roles and attendance for the outlet, without going near the ERP.

Step 25 · The manager's day

Day close

Count each tender, see the variance per tender, print the Z report. A venue closing at 3am gets one business day and one close, not two halves that can never be reconciled.

Failures are stated, not hidden.

One thing this walkthrough cannot show you, because we will not fake a screenshot to fill a gap: an action that could not be completed says so, in the operator's words, with what to do next. A write whose outcome is unknown is never silently retried — it is handed back to a person, because the alternative is charging a customer twice.
